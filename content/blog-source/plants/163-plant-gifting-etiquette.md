# Plant Gifting Etiquette: Do's and Don'ts

A few small missteps come up repeatedly in plant gifting, most avoidable by matching the plant to the recipient's actual space, experience, and occasion.

## Do: Consider Where They Live and Work

A small apartment is better served by a compact plant than a large areca palm; a home office suits a bonsai or jade plant well. Matching the plant to the actual space matters more here than for most other gift categories.

## Don't: Default to a Demanding Plant for an Inexperienced Recipient

First-time plant owners should receive low-maintenance options — money plant, snake plant, pothos, syngonium — rather than something like bonsai that requires more consistent attention.

## Do: Match Symbolism to the Occasion

Prosperity and luck point to money plant or jade; calm and care point to peace lily; art and patience point to bonsai — choosing with this in mind adds meaning a generic choice doesn't carry.

## Don't: Assume Every Occasion Calls for the Same Plant

Just as flower and cake choice varies by occasion, plant choice should too — a snake plant that suits a new colleague doesn't carry the same weight as a bonsai chosen for a retirement.

## Quick Answers

**What's the most common plant gifting mistake?**
Choosing a plant that doesn't fit the recipient's actual space or experience level, rather than considering these factors before selecting.

**Should plant symbolism factor into the choice?**
Yes — matching symbolism to occasion (prosperity, calm, patience, growth) adds meaning that a generic choice doesn't provide.
