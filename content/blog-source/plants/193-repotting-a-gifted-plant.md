# Repotting a Gifted Plant: When and How

A gifted plant doesn't need to be repotted immediately, and doing so too early or unnecessarily can actually stress the plant rather than help it.

## Signs a Plant Actually Needs Repotting

Roots visibly growing out of the drainage holes, water running straight through without being absorbed, or a plant that's outgrown its pot's proportions are the clearest signs repotting is genuinely needed — not simply the fact that time has passed since receiving the gift.

## When to Wait Before Repotting

A newly gifted plant benefits from a settling-in period in its original pot before any repotting, since the transition from its growing environment to a new home is already a mild stress on its own without adding a pot change at the same time.

## Choosing a New Pot Size

When repotting is genuinely needed, choosing a pot only slightly larger than the current one — rather than a dramatically bigger size — helps the plant adjust without the excess soil retaining more water than the smaller root system can use.

## Quick Answers

**Should I repot a plant as soon as I receive it as a gift?**
Not necessarily — allowing a settling-in period in the original pot is generally better than repotting immediately.

**How do I know when a plant actually needs repotting?**
Roots growing out of drainage holes, water running through without absorption, or a plant clearly outgrowing its pot's proportions are the clearest signs.
