# Which Common Gift Plants Are Safe for Homes With Pets?

This is worth checking carefully before gifting a plant to someone with cats or dogs, since several of the most commonly recommended gift plants are not actually pet-safe — a detail that's easy to miss since it isn't always mentioned alongside their popularity.

## Common Gift Plants That Carry Pet Toxicity Risk

According to the ASPCA's toxic plant database, several familiar low-maintenance favorites carry real risk: peace lily, pothos (also commonly sold in India as "money plant"), snake plant, and jade plant are all listed as toxic to cats and dogs if chewed or ingested. For most of these, the mechanism is calcium oxalate crystals or similar compounds causing oral irritation, drooling, and gastrointestinal upset — generally mild to moderate, and rarely life-threatening, but a genuine concern for a curious pet that chews on houseplants.

## An Important Distinction: Peace Lily vs. True Lilies

It's worth being precise here, since the word "lily" covers very different risk levels. Peace lily's toxicity is mild-to-moderate GI and oral irritation. True lilies — Easter, tiger, Asiatic, and similar Lilium species, which show up in cut flower arrangements rather than as houseplants — are a different and far more serious matter: even small ingestions can cause acute kidney failure and death in cats specifically. If a household has cats, avoiding true lily cut flowers matters considerably more than worrying about a peace lily plant.

## Plants Generally Considered Safe for Pets

Areca palm, spider plant, and Boston fern are commonly cited as non-toxic according to ASPCA's database, making them safer defaults for a pet-owning household than the usual low-maintenance recommendations.

## What This Means for Gifting Decisions

If you know the recipient has cats or dogs — particularly pets known to chew on plants — it's worth choosing from the genuinely pet-safe list rather than defaulting to snake plant or pothos out of habit, even though these are frequently recommended for their low-maintenance qualities elsewhere. If you don't know whether the recipient has pets, mentioning the plant's status alongside the gift, or choosing a pet-safe option as a precaution, is a reasonable extra step.

## Quick Answers

**Is snake plant safe for homes with cats or dogs?**
No — it's listed on the ASPCA's toxic plant database, generally causing mild gastrointestinal upset if ingested, though severe reactions are uncommon.

**Is peace lily as dangerous as true lilies for cats?**
No — peace lily causes milder oral and GI irritation, while true lilies (Easter, tiger, Asiatic) can cause acute kidney failure and death in cats even from small ingestions. These are different plants despite the shared "lily" name.
