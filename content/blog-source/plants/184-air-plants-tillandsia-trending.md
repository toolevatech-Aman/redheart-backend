# Air Plants (Tillandsia): What They Are and Why They're Trending

Air plants represent a genuinely different category from typical gift plants — they grow without soil at all, drawing moisture and nutrients directly from the air around them.

## What Makes Air Plants Distinctive

Since they don't require soil, air plants can be displayed in unconventional ways — mounted on a piece of wood, placed in an open glass vessel, or simply set on a shelf — offering a more flexible, design-forward presentation than a standard potted plant.

## Why They've Grown Popular in Gifting

Their unusual growing method and flexible display options appeal to recipients looking for something visually distinctive beyond the familiar money plant or snake plant defaults, fitting a broader trend toward design-conscious, curated plant gifting.

## Care Considerations

Air plants need periodic misting or soaking rather than traditional watering, a different care routine worth explaining to a recipient unfamiliar with this plant type, since watering it like a standard potted plant would be the wrong approach.

## Quick Answers

**Do air plants need soil?**
No — they draw moisture and nutrients from the air, allowing them to be displayed without a traditional pot.

**How do you water an air plant?**
Through periodic misting or soaking, rather than the traditional watering routine used for soil-based plants.
