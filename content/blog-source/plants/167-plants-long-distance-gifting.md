# Plants for Long-Distance Gifting: Do They Survive Transit?

Sending a living plant across cities raises a reasonable question flowers and cake don't face in quite the same way — will it actually survive the trip in good condition?

## How Plants Are Prepared for Transit

Every plant is carefully packaged — soil secured, pot protected, leaves unbruised — to ensure it arrives in the same condition it left in, addressing the specific risks (soil spillage, leaf damage, pot breakage) that transit introduces.

## Why Plants Are Actually Well Suited to Distance Gifting

Unlike flowers, which are actively fading from the moment they're cut, a properly packaged plant isn't degrading during transit the same way — it's simply paused in transport rather than racing against a freshness clock, making distance less of a quality concern than it is for flowers.

## Choosing Sturdier Plants for Longer Transit

For particularly long-distance deliveries, hardier plants like snake plant or jade tend to tolerate transit disruption better than more delicate flowering plants like peace lily, which can be slightly more sensitive to handling stress during longer transport.

## Quick Answers

**Do plants survive long-distance delivery well?**
Yes, generally — proper packaging protects against the main transit risks (soil spillage, leaf damage), and plants aren't racing against a freshness clock the way cut flowers are.

**Which plants are best suited to longer transit specifically?**
Hardier options like snake plant or jade tend to tolerate transit disruption better than more delicate flowering plants.
