# A Simple Guide to Indoor Plant Care for Gift Recipients

Receiving a plant as a gift comes with a small, unspoken responsibility that a bouquet doesn't — keeping it alive. Most gifted plants aren't fussy, but a few basic habits make the difference between a plant that thrives for years and one that quietly declines within a few months of arriving.

## Light: Match the Plant to Where It Actually Sits

**Bright, indirect light** suits most common gift plants — money plant, jade, and pothos generally do well near a window that gets good daylight without harsh direct sun hitting the leaves for hours at a time.

**Low-light tolerant plants** — snake plants in particular — are forgiving of dimmer spots, like an interior room or a desk away from windows, which is part of why they're such a common office and apartment gift; they don't demand ideal conditions to survive.

**Avoid assuming more light is always better.** Direct, intense sunlight for extended periods can scorch the leaves of plants that actually prefer indirect light, even though it seems like giving a plant "more light" should help it thrive.

## Watering: The Most Common Way Gift Plants Are Overcared For

Overwatering, not underwatering, is the most common cause of a gifted plant declining. Most common indoor plants — money plant, jade, snake plant, pothos — actually prefer to dry out somewhat between waterings rather than staying constantly moist. A simple check: press a finger into the top inch of soil, and only water if it feels dry rather than watering on a fixed daily schedule regardless of soil condition.

**Jade and snake plants in particular** are succulent-adjacent and store water in their leaves, making them especially prone to root rot if watered too frequently. Underwatering is a much safer mistake to make with these varieties than overwatering.

**Peace lilies** are a partial exception — they're more sensitive to drying out completely and will visibly droop as a signal that they need water, which actually makes them easier to read than plants that don't show obvious distress signs before real damage occurs.

## Drainage Matters as Much as Watering Frequency

A pot without proper drainage holes can cause problems even if watering habits are otherwise correct, since excess water has nowhere to go and can sit around the roots, encouraging rot. If a gifted plant arrived in a decorative pot without drainage, it's often worth keeping the plant in its original nursery pot (with drainage) and placing that inside the decorative one, removing any water that collects in the bottom after watering.

## Basic Care by Common Gift Plant

**Money plant** — thrives in indirect light, tolerates some neglect, and can even grow in water alone for extended periods, making it one of the most forgiving common gift plants.

**Jade plant** — needs bright light and infrequent watering; allow soil to dry out significantly between waterings.

**Snake plant** — extremely low-maintenance, tolerates low light and irregular watering, making it one of the hardest common gift plants to accidentally kill.

**Peace lily** — prefers consistent moderate moisture and indirect light; will droop visibly when it needs water, which serves as a helpful natural warning sign.

**Syngonium and pothos** — both do well in indirect light with soil allowed to dry slightly between waterings; both also tolerate occasional inconsistency in care reasonably well.

**Bonsai** — generally requires more attentive, consistent care than the other plants on this list, including regular light watering and occasional pruning; worth researching the specific variety received, since bonsai care varies more by species than most other common gift plants.

## Signs a Plant Needs Attention

**Yellowing leaves** often indicate overwatering rather than underwatering, despite the common assumption that yellowing means the plant needs more water.

**Brown, crispy leaf edges** typically point to underwatering, too much direct sun, or low humidity.

**Drooping without recovery after watering** can indicate root rot from prior overwatering, which is harder to reverse than simple dehydration.

## Quick Answers

**What's the most common mistake people make with gifted plants?**
Overwatering — most common indoor gift plants prefer to dry out somewhat between waterings, and watering on a fixed schedule regardless of soil condition is a common cause of decline.

**Which gifted plants are hardest to kill?**
Snake plants and money plants are generally the most forgiving, tolerating irregular watering and a range of light conditions.

**How do I know if my peace lily needs water?**
It will visibly droop, which serves as a natural, fairly reliable signal that it's time to water, unlike some plants that show distress only after significant damage has occurred.

**Should I repot a plant that arrived in a decorative pot without drainage holes?**
It's often better to keep the plant in its original nursery pot with drainage and place that inside the decorative pot, rather than repotting directly into a container without drainage.
