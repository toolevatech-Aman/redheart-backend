# How to Revive a Struggling Gifted Plant

A gifted plant showing signs of distress isn't necessarily beyond saving — identifying the actual cause matters more than reacting with more general care, which can sometimes make things worse.

## Diagnosing the Problem First

Yellowing leaves often indicate overwatering rather than underwatering, despite the common instinct to add more water when a plant looks unwell. Brown, crispy leaf edges typically point to underwatering, too much direct sun, or low humidity — a different diagnosis requiring a different fix.

## If Overwatering Is the Cause

Allowing the soil to dry out more fully between waterings, and checking that the pot has proper drainage, addresses overwatering directly — adding less water going forward rather than continuing a fixed watering schedule regardless of soil condition.

## If Underwatering or Light Issues Are the Cause

Increasing watering frequency slightly, or moving the plant to a location with more appropriate indirect light, addresses these causes — though changes should be gradual rather than dramatic, since sudden shifts in light or watering can add further stress to an already struggling plant.

## When a Plant May Be Too Far Gone

Drooping that doesn't recover after adjusting care, combined with soft, blackened stems, can indicate root rot that's progressed too far to reverse — in this case, taking a cutting (if the plant type allows) to propagate a new plant may be more successful than trying to save the original.

## Quick Answers

**Does yellowing always mean a plant needs more water?**
No — yellowing more often indicates overwatering, despite the common instinct to respond by watering more.

**Can a plant with root rot be saved?**
Sometimes, if caught early by improving drainage and reducing watering; if it's progressed to soft, blackened stems, propagating a new plant from a cutting may be more successful than saving the original.
