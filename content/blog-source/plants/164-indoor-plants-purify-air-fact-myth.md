# Indoor Plants That Purify Air: Fact or Myth?

This claim gets repeated often enough in plant gifting and care content that it's worth looking at honestly, since the science is more contested than the popular version of the story suggests.

## Where the Claim Comes From

The idea traces back to a 1989 NASA study that tested whether certain houseplants could remove volatile organic compounds (VOCs) like formaldehyde and benzene from sealed, enclosed environments — originally researched for potential use in space station life-support systems, not home décor.

## Why the Real-World Application Is Disputed

The NASA study measured plants in small, sealed chambers, conditions quite different from an actual home or office with normal air exchange through doors, windows, and ventilation. A widely cited 2019 analysis by researchers Bryan Cummings and Michael Waring found that natural air exchange in typical rooms outpaces what a realistic number of houseplants could filter, concluding that achieving a meaningful effect would require an impractically dense number of plants per square meter. Organizations including the American Lung Association have since stated that houseplants do not meaningfully improve indoor air quality in real living spaces, based on this and similar research.

## What This Means for Plant Gifting

The air-purifying claim, while not fabricated, is generally overstated when applied to a typical home or office rather than the sealed conditions it was originally tested under. This doesn't diminish plants as gifts — their genuine benefits (mood, aesthetics, the symbolic and lasting qualities that make them meaningful gifts) stand on their own without needing the air-purification claim to justify them.

## A More Accurate Way to Frame It

Plants are worth gifting and enjoying for their visual presence, the sense of care and growth they represent, and genuine associations with improved mood and wellbeing — rather than as a substitute for ventilation or an actual air purifier, which remain more effective solutions for air quality specifically.

## Quick Answers

**Do houseplants really purify indoor air?**
The science is contested — the original NASA study measured effects in sealed chambers, and more recent research suggests the effect is too small to matter meaningfully in a typical home with normal air exchange.

**Should I still value plants for their air-related benefits?**
It's more accurate to appreciate plants for their aesthetic and mood benefits rather than relying on them for measurable air purification, which ventilation and air purifiers handle more effectively.
