# Why Flower Gifting Means So Much in Amritsar

Amritsar's identity as home to the Golden Temple, the holiest site in Sikhism, gives the city a devotional character that shapes its flower culture more than almost any single landmark shapes a city elsewhere in India.

## The Golden Temple's Daily Devotional Pull

The Golden Temple, or Harmandir Sahib, draws devotees and visitors continuously throughout the year, not just during specific festivals — a pattern that sustains genuine daily flower demand tied to devotional offerings and decoration around the temple complex, distinct from purely occasion-driven purchasing found in many other cities.

## Gurpurabs as Amritsar's Defining Occasions

Gurpurabs, commemorating significant events in Sikh history, hold particular significance in Amritsar specifically, given the city's status as Sikhism's holiest city — these occasions carry more concentrated local weight here than in most other Indian cities observing the same commemorations.

## Baisakhi's Deep Punjab Roots

Baisakhi carries major significance in Amritsar, tied to both Punjab's agricultural harvest traditions and its specific importance in Sikh history, given the festival's connection to the founding of the Khalsa.

## Lohri and Diwali Within the Punjab Calendar

Alongside its distinctive Sikh devotional character, Amritsar observes Lohri and Diwali consistent with Punjab's broader festival traditions, adding these more widely shared occasions to the city's own deeply devotional calendar.

## What This Means for Choosing a Gift

Given how central the Golden Temple and Sikh devotional life are to Amritsar's identity, traditional flowers suited to devotional offerings — marigold especially — tend to align with the city's own gifting sensibility for occasions with any religious dimension.
