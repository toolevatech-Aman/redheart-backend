# Why Flower Gifting Means So Much in Chandigarh

Chandigarh's identity as India's first planned city, designed by the renowned architect Le Corbusier, gives it a distinct character built around order, greenery, and deliberate design — qualities that show up in the city's own well-known love of gardens and flowers.

## A City Designed Around Green Space

Chandigarh's urban planning deliberately incorporated extensive green belts and gardens into the city's layout from its founding, most famously realized in the Zakir Hussain Rose Garden, one of the largest rose gardens in Asia — giving the city a genuine, built-in relationship with flowers as part of its civic identity rather than something separate from it.

## A City Shared Between Punjab and Haryana's Traditions

As the shared capital of Punjab and Haryana, Chandigarh's festival calendar draws on both states' traditions — Lohri, Baisakhi, and Diwali all carry significant weight here, consistent with the broader North Indian and Punjabi festival calendar found in nearby Punjab cities.

## A Well-Educated, Design-Conscious Population

Chandigarh's reputation for high livability and a well-planned urban environment has attracted a population with a genuine appreciation for design and aesthetic presentation — a quality that reasonably extends to how the city's residents approach gifting and celebration.

## What This Means for Choosing a Gift

Given Chandigarh's built-in relationship with flowers through its famous rose gardens and deliberate urban greenery, and its Punjab-Haryana festival traditions, choosing well-presented flowers — particularly roses, given the city's own connection to the Zakir Hussain Rose Garden — tends to align naturally with local sensibility.
