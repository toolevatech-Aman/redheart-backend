# Chennai's Flower Markets and What They Tell You About Local Taste

Chennai's flower trade runs through markets built around daily religious demand as much as gift-giving, giving the city's supply chain a rhythm distinct from cities where flower buying spikes mainly around specific occasions.

## Koyambedu Market: Asia's Scale Applied to Daily Ritual

Koyambedu Market is one of the largest wholesale markets in the country, and its flower section supplies not just gift-oriented buyers but the enormous daily demand tied to temple offerings and personal use across the city. This steady, ritual-driven baseline demand gives Chennai's flower trade a more consistent daily rhythm than cities where demand spikes sharply around specific festivals or occasions.

## Mylapore's Neighborhood Flower Culture

Beyond the large-scale wholesale trade at Koyambedu, Mylapore's flower stalls represent a more neighborhood-scale, walkable flower-buying culture centered around the area's significant temple presence — a reminder that Chennai's flower economy operates at both an industrial wholesale scale and a smaller, community level simultaneously.

## What Moves Through in the Highest Volume

Jasmine (mullai), roses, marigolds, tuberose, chrysanthemums, and lotuses dominate Chennai's flower trade — a broader traditional range than many other Indian cities, reflecting the depth of religious and cultural flower use here. Roses represent the more contemporary, gift-specific segment of this mix.
