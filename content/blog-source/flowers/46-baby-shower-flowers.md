# Baby Shower Flowers: What to Send Expecting Parents

A baby shower gift has a specific tone to strike — celebratory and warm, but softer than most other party occasions, since the focus is on anticipation and new beginnings rather than an achievement already accomplished.

## The Right Color Palette for a Baby Shower

**Soft pastels** — pale pinks, light blues, gentle yellows and greens — are the most natural fit for baby shower flowers, echoing the same soft, hopeful tone that baby shower decor generally leans into. This is one of the few occasions where color choice is driven more by tone than by any deeper symbolic meaning.

**Neutral tones (soft yellows, whites, light greens)** work well when the baby's gender isn't known or isn't being revealed at the shower, avoiding the pink-or-blue assumption before the parents have shared that information themselves.

**Once gender is known and being celebrated**, leaning into pink or blue more directly is generally welcome and expected, particularly for showers built around a gender-reveal theme.

## Flower Types That Suit the Occasion

**Mixed pastel bouquets** work broadly well, since they capture the soft, celebratory tone without requiring a single dominant flower type.

**Roses in soft pink or white** suit a slightly more elegant baby shower, especially one with a more refined, less playful decor theme.

**Gerberas and daisies** suit a more playful, casual shower — their naturally cheerful appearance fits an occasion that's meant to feel joyful and light rather than formal.

## Should You Send Flowers or a Plant?

Flowers work well as a shower-day gesture — something that adds visual warmth to the event itself or arrives as part of a gift for the parents-to-be. For something with more staying power, a plant can work as an addition or alternative, following similar logic to other gifting occasions where longevity matters — a low-maintenance plant can be a small, ongoing part of the nursery or living space as the family settles into their new routine.

## Combining Flowers With a Practical Gift

Since baby showers are typically organized around practical gift-giving (registry items, baby essentials), flowers work best as a complement rather than the primary gift — a small bouquet accompanying a more practical present rounds out the gesture, adding a visual and celebratory element to what might otherwise be a purely functional gift.

## Sending Flowers If You Can't Attend

For guests who can't attend the shower in person, sending flowers directly to the expecting parents on or around the shower date is a thoughtful way to participate from a distance — similar in spirit to sending wedding flowers when you can't attend the ceremony itself. A vase arrangement works well here, since it arrives ready to display without requiring the parents-to-be to manage additional logistics during an already busy time.

## What to Avoid

**Overly bold, saturated colors** that clash with the soft, anticipatory tone most baby showers are built around — this isn't the occasion for the same intensity that might suit a birthday or celebratory achievement.

**Assuming gender before it's been shared.** Neutral tones are the safer default until the parents have specifically revealed or confirmed the baby's gender.

**Heavily fragrant arrangements**, which can be uncomfortable for an expecting mother, particularly if she's sensitive to strong scents during pregnancy — a gentler, lower-fragrance bouquet is generally the safer choice.

## Quick Answers

**What color flowers are appropriate for a baby shower?**
Soft pastels — pale pinks, light blues, gentle yellows and greens — suit the occasion's soft, celebratory tone. Neutral tones are safest if the baby's gender hasn't been revealed.

**Should baby shower flowers be strongly scented?**
No — gentler, lower-fragrance arrangements are generally safer, since strong scents can be uncomfortable for an expecting mother.

**Is it better to send flowers or a practical gift for a baby shower?**
Flowers work best as a complement to a practical gift (registry items, baby essentials) rather than as the primary present, since baby showers are typically organized around functional giving.

**Can I send flowers if I can't attend the baby shower?**
Yes — sending a vase arrangement directly to the expecting parents around the shower date is a thoughtful way to participate from a distance without requiring them to manage extra logistics.
