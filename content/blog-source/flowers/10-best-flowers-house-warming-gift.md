# Best Flowers (and Plants) for a House Warming Gift

A house warming gift has a specific job that most other occasion gifts don't: it has to fit into a space the recipient is still figuring out. Flowers alone can feel slightly thin for this occasion — beautiful for a week, then gone, in a home that's meant to represent a longer-term beginning. This is one of the few occasions where the plant, not the flower, tends to be the stronger default.

## Why Plants Outperform Flowers for This Occasion Specifically

When someone moves into a new home, they're starting something — and a plant mirrors that in a way cut flowers can't. It grows as the home grows, rather than peaking on arrival and fading within days. That symbolic fit is a big part of why plants have become the go-to house warming gift over the last several years, alongside the more practical reality that a new homeowner often appreciates something that adds life to a still-sparse space more than something that will need to be discarded in a week.

## The Two Most Traditional Choices: Money Plant and Jade

Money plant and jade are the most popular house warming plants, and both carry prosperity associations that are especially meaningful in the context of a new home — the implicit sentiment being a wish for good fortune to grow alongside the household itself. Both are also relatively low-maintenance, which matters for a recipient who may be too occupied with moving logistics to manage a demanding plant in the first few weeks.

## Other Strong Options Depending on the Space

**Peace lily** adds a touch of elegance beyond the prosperity symbolism of money plant or jade — a good choice if you want the gift to feel a little more refined, or if the recipient's new home leans toward a more polished aesthetic.

**Snake plant** makes more of a visual statement — taller, structural, and a good fit for a living room or entryway where you want the plant to actually be noticed rather than sit quietly on a shelf. It's also genuinely low-maintenance, which suits a recipient who's admitted they don't have a track record with plants.

**Consider the space before choosing.** A small apartment is generally better served by a compact plant — a snake plant or syngonium — than something larger like an areca palm, which needs more room and light than many first apartments can offer.

## Should You Send Flowers Instead, or in Addition?

Flowers aren't wrong for a house warming — they add immediate color and warmth to a space the recipient is still furnishing, and a soft mixed bouquet in warm tones can make a new, half-empty home feel more lived-in from day one. If you want to combine both, a house warming hamper — flowers, a small cake, and a money plant or jade — covers the immediate visual warmth, the celebratory element, and the lasting symbolism in a single delivery.

## What to Avoid

**Anything that requires a lot of light or specific care**, unless you know the recipient's new home has the right conditions — an unfamiliar space is not the moment to gift a demanding plant that needs bright, direct sun the apartment may not get.

**Overly large arrangements**, if the home is still being set up. A new resident may not yet have a place to display a very large bouquet or plant; something modest and easy to place tends to be more practically appreciated in the first weeks.
