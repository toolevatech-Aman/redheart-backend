# Ordering Cake for a Colleague You Don't Know Well

Choosing a cake for someone whose specific preferences you don't know calls for defaulting to broadly safe choices rather than guessing at something more specific.

## Why Broad Appeal Matters More Than Personalization Here

Without a known flavor preference, prioritizing broadly liked options over a more personalized guess reduces the risk of choosing something the recipient genuinely dislikes — a reasonable default is more reliable than an uninformed personal guess.

## The Safest Flavor Defaults

Chocolate or Black Forest generally serve as the safest defaults, given their broad appeal across most palates, mirroring the same logic that governs safe flower color choices when a recipient's preference is unknown.

## Considering Dietary Requirements Without Asking Directly

Defaulting to eggless, given how common the requirement is across Indian workplaces, is a reasonable precaution even without knowing the specific colleague's dietary practices — it's the lower-risk choice when uncertain.

## Keeping the Gesture Modest

For a colleague you don't know especially well, a modest, appropriately scaled cake avoids the gesture feeling disproportionate to the relationship, following the same restraint that governs professional gifting broadly.
