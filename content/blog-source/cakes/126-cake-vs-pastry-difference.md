# Cake vs Pastry: What's the Difference?

These terms sometimes get used loosely, but they refer to genuinely different things — worth understanding if you're deciding between a full cake and individual pastries for a gifting occasion.

## What Defines a Cake

A cake is typically a single, complete dessert built to be sliced and shared — sized to serve a specific number of people, and generally the centerpiece of a celebration in a way individual pastries aren't designed to be.

## What Defines a Pastry

Pastries are individual, smaller desserts — often flaky or layered, and designed to be enjoyed as a single serving rather than sliced and shared from a larger whole. They suit different gifting contexts than cake, often working better as part of a varied assortment than as a singular occasion centerpiece.

## When to Choose Cake Over Pastries

For a clear, singular occasion — a birthday, an anniversary — a cake remains the more traditional and expected centerpiece, providing the shared, communal cutting moment pastries don't replicate.

## When to Choose Pastries Over Cake

For a gathering where variety matters more than a singular centerpiece, or for individual gifting where a full cake would be excessive for one person, an assortment of pastries can work better — offering choice without the commitment of a full cake.

## Quick Answers

**What's the main difference between a cake and pastries?**
A cake is a single, complete dessert sized to be sliced and shared, while pastries are individual, smaller desserts meant to be enjoyed as single servings.

**When should I choose pastries instead of a cake?**
For gatherings where variety matters more than a singular centerpiece, or for individual gifting where a full cake would be more than needed.
