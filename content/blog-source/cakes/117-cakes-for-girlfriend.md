# Cakes for Your Girlfriend

Cake flavor for a girlfriend can follow the same relationship-stage calibration used for flower color choice — red velvet's romantic intensity suits an established relationship better than a very new one.

## Calibrating by Relationship Stage

Early on, a lighter flavor — vanilla or a mild option — avoids the intensity red velvet carries, mirroring the same softer-color logic used for early-relationship flowers. As the relationship becomes more established, red velvet becomes an appropriate, even expected, choice.

## Personal Preference Still Wins

If she has a known favorite flavor, using it outranks any relationship-stage convention, the same principle that governs flower choice throughout romantic gifting.

## Pairing With Flowers for a Complete Gesture

Matching cake flavor to flower color — red velvet with red roses for established relationships, a lighter cake with pink roses for newer ones — creates a cohesive combined gift.

## Quick Answers

**Should I avoid red velvet early in a relationship?**
It can feel like too much too soon, similar to how deep red roses can feel premature — a lighter flavor is often a safer early choice.

**What matters more: relationship stage or her personal preference?**
Personal preference generally outranks convention when known — the stage-based guidance is mainly useful when her specific taste isn't yet known.
